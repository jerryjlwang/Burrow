Description

This assignment is the fourth in our equations unit and closes out the work we started with balance puzzles. Over the past two weeks you have practiced keeping an equation balanced by doing the same thing to both sides, first with pictures of scales and blocks, then with numbers, and finally with a variable standing in for the unknown. Assignment 4 asks you to put all of that together: each problem is a linear equation that can be solved in two or three moves, and the point is not just the answer but the record of the moves themselves. That is why the notes box matters — writing "subtract 5 from both sides" next to the line where you did it is exactly the kind of reasoning we are grading. When you check an answer on the practice page, the checker only tells you whether the value works; it does not tell you whether your steps were legal, so read your own working line by line before you submit. A common trap in this set is moving a term across the equals sign and forgetting to flip its sign, so watch for that. Calculators are allowed but will not help much, since every problem uses small whole numbers on purpose. If you finish early, there is a hidden challenge: one of the practice problems has coefficients taken from the Fibonacci sequence, and spotting it earns a golden ratio bonus of two extra credit points on this assignment. Late work is accepted until Sunday night with a small deduction, and you can resubmit once before grades post on Tuesday. If anything here is unclear, leave a note for your teacher in the box below or ask during Wednesday's help session.
